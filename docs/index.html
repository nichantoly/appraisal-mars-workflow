03Market

Market sales (the pool)

One CSV export: every closed sale of the subject's property type in its market, over the whole window you need (up to six years is fine). Every clean sale in it builds the time index; the similarity ranges in step 04 pick which of them price the other lines. Bright MLS (Spark) column names map on their own. 05Run

Run the workup

One run weighs every listing against what is typical for its size, builds the time index from every clean sale, brings the adjustment pool to the effective date, fits the MARS model and refits it with weights, cross-validates it, and refits the whole thing on 200 random halves of the data to see which lines hold up. A thousand sales took about ten seconds in testing; an older computer takes longer.

06Results

Method, validation and limits

Lines. The grid prices site (in dollars per square foot of lot), bedrooms, full baths and half baths (all above grade: the MLS total minus the lower-level count), GLA, garage spaces, carport spaces and pool, plus a percent time line. Year built, levels and finished basement (yes or no) stay in the model only as background controls, so they cannot leak into the priced lines. A line needs at least 10 pool sales that differ from the most common value; with fewer it reads Not enough data. Basement area and rooms, decks, porches, fireplaces, concessions and location are left to the appraiser.

Model. Multivariate adaptive regression splines (Friedman, 1991), reimplemented in JavaScript from the source of the R earth package by Stephen Milborrow: forward pass, knot search, GCV backward pruning. The browser engine matches R earth on all 71 test fits. MARS finds where each curve bends; any priced variable it leaves out goes back in as a straight line, so every line gets a number and a range. Settings and the adjustment convention (value contribution of the subject minus the comp, per variable) follow W. Craytor's open-source earthUI.

Weights. A bedroom, bath, garage or carport count more than 2 from what is typical for the sale's size (the median count among the nearest 20% of the sales by GLA, at least 30 sales) counts for less: weight 2 divided by the distance, so a recorded 36-car garage on a 2-car street counts about 6%. A finished basement with its lower-level rooms left blank and more rooms than typical on the main line gets half weight on top. Sales priced far from what the model expects get Huber weights. The dollar amounts come from the weighted refit; nothing is dropped for any of this, and every listing that lost weight is listed.

Time. Market conditions come from a separate quality-adjusted log-price index fit on every clean sale in the export: log price on the model variables and quarter of the year, plus a time curve that may bend only on a calendar grid (Jan 1 and Jul 1 by default) where at least 20 sales sit on each side (10 in the half-sample refits, which use half the sales), with bends pruned by GCV and the same weights. Prices in the adjustment pool are brought to the effective date with it before the feature lines are priced, and each comp's time line is its price times the index change.

Uncertainty and validation. Each line carries a half-sample range: the full procedure refit on 200 random halves of the sales, so knots, variable choices and weights are allowed to move. In 16 synthetic markets with known true values, four price paths and realtor-style listing errors mixed in (320 lines), that range held the true amount 96% of the time and every line rated Supported had the right sign; the textbook ±1.96 SE interval held it 64% of the time. With the weights switched off the typical miss grew on 8 of the 9 line types. The R reference script matches this page line for line on 10 test cases, weights, folds, half-samples and every run note included.

Status rules and limits. Supported: same sign in at least 90% of the refits. Size variable: at least 60%. Unstable: under 60%. Lines are also flagged when the comp or subject sits outside the modeled sales, when a segment runs against the normal market direction, when FHFA's index disagrees with this export's index over the same years, when leaving your comps out moves a line outside its range, and when the full-data amount falls outside its own half-sample range. The model only sees what the MLS records: condition, quality, view and finish are not in the data. This page produces adjustment support only: no indicated, reconciled or opinion-of-value figure.
