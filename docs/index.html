Effective date and data rules

The effective date drives sale age, the time adjustment, and which sales were knowable. For a retrospective date (date of separation, filing or trial) enter that date: every sale that closed after it drops out, and so does anything you could only have learned later.

Run the workup

One run builds the time index from every clean sale, brings the adjustment pool to the effective date, fits the MARS model, cross-validates it, and refits both on 200 random halves of the data to see which adjustments hold up. Several seconds for a thousand sales.

06Results

Method, validation and limits

Model. Multivariate adaptive regression splines (Friedman, 1991), reimplemented in JavaScript from the source of the R earth package by Stephen Milborrow: forward pass, knot search, GCV backward pruning. Settings, the adjustment convention (value contribution of the subject minus the comp, per variable) and the residual pricing of rare amenities follow W. Craytor's open-source earthUI. The browser engine matches R earth on all 71 test fits, including a 1,488-sale fit on earthUI's demo file, and in every test case it matched the open R reference script line for line, folds and half-samples included.

Time. Market conditions come from a separate quality-adjusted log-price index fit on every clean sale in the export: log price on the model features, location and quarter of the year, plus a time curve that may bend only on a calendar grid (Jan 1 and Jul 1 by default) where at least 20 sales sit on each side, with bends pruned by GCV. Prices in the adjustment pool are brought to the effective date with it before MARS prices the features, and each comp's time line is its price times the index change. In 32 synthetic markets with known trends (160 checks), the time line's half-sample range held the true change every time: treat it as a conservative range.

Uncertainty. Each line carries a half-sample range: the full procedure refit on 200 random halves of the sales, so the knots and the variable choices are allowed to move. In 10 synthetic markets with known true adjustments (285 lines) that range held the true amount 94% of the time; the textbook ±1.96 SE interval held it 56% of the time. Every line rated Supported or Size variable had the right sign.

Status rules. Supported: same sign in at least 90% of the refits. Size variable: at least 60%. Unstable: under 60%. Lines are also flagged when the comp or subject sits outside the modeled sales (extrapolation), when a segment runs against the normal market direction, when FHFA's index disagrees with this export's index over the same years, and when leaving your comps out of the fit moves a line outside its range.

Limits. The model only sees what the MLS records. Condition, quality, view and finish are not in the data, which is why the comp check shows how far each comp sold above or below the model. The degree-2 interaction check runs in the R reference script, not on this page. This page produces adjustment support only: no indicated, reconciled or opinion-of-value figure.
