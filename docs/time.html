Time Adjustment Support

Market-conditions support for a comp of any age, from one broad MLS export. Four methods side by side, in percent: this export's quality-adjusted index (the same index the MARS Adjustment Support tool uses), a repeat-sales index built from the export's own resales, yearly medians, and FHFA. Set a price tier and every method also runs on the sales in that slice of each year's prices, so an upper-end comp is read against the upper end of the market, not the whole county.

Effective date and the comps that need time support

Every figure runs from a comp's sale to this date. For a retrospective date (date of separation, filing or trial), enter that date: every sale that closed after it drops out. A comp can be any age, as long as the export reaches back to its sale.

04Run

Run the time workup

One run cleans the export with the main tool's rules, fits the quality-adjusted index on every clean sale (and on the tier when it is set), refits each index on 200 random halves of the sales, builds the repeat-sales index from the export's own same-house pairs with 200 bootstrap draws, lines up the yearly medians and FHFA, and reads every comp against all of them. A 5,000-row export with the tier set took about a minute in testing; an older computer takes longer.

Method, validation and limits

Quality-adjusted index. Log price on GLA, site, bedrooms, baths, garage, carport, pool, year built, levels, finished basement and location, plus a time curve that may bend only on set calendar dates (Jan 1 and Jul 1 by default) where at least 20 sales sit on each side, with bends pruned by GCV. Listings far from typical for their size and prices far from the model count for less. It is the main tool's time index, same code and same settings: on the same export with location off, the two match month by month. Here location is on by default, because a 14-year export usually spans more ground. Its range is what 200 refits on random halves of the sales span.

Price tier. The sales between two percentiles of their own year's clean sales, recut every year, so the tier follows the market instead of a fixed dollar floor. The tier index is the same model fit on the tier alone, with its own half-sample refits. A comp inside the tier for its year is read against the tier index, any other comp against the all-sales index.

Repeat sales. Pairs of clean sales of the same property (Tax ID, else address) in different years, with no GLA change over 5% and no change in bedroom or bath count between them: the log of the later price over the earlier one, on yearly periods, least squares with no intercept (Bailey, Muth and Nourse). Its range comes from 200 bootstrap draws of the pairs. Renovations between sales that leave size and room count alone still read as appreciation, so this index tends to run high.

Yearly medians and FHFA. Median closed price by closing year, every closed sale as pulled (and the tier's own sales), from each year to the base year: the effective date's year, or the latest year with sales. FHFA's change runs over its own periods. Each check is compared with the index over the same span: years against years, FHFA periods against the same periods.

Validation and limits. In 10 synthetic 14-year exports where upper-end homes followed their own price path, salted with realtor-style listing errors, flips, REO sales and renovations the MLS never records, the tier index (85th to 100th percentile) held the upper tier's true change inside its half-sample range at 143 of 150 comp dates from 2012 to 2026 (95%), with a median miss of 3.1 points; read against the all-sales index the same comps held at 71 (47%). In 10 one-path exports where bigger homes sold more often in the 2020 to 2022 run-up, the all-sales index held the truth at 144 of 150 dates (96%), median miss 1.2 points, while yearly medians missed by 4.6 points at the median and by up to 23. The unrecorded renovations pushed every method high: the tier index ran 3.8 points high on average at the comp dates, and repeat sales 2 to 3 points high over the same years. Tier medians missed the most, 10 points at the median and far more in years with few tier sales. This page runs in the browser only; there is no R script for it yet. Its all-sales index is the main tool's time index, which the main tool's R reference script reproduces. The index measures quality-held-constant change in this export; it cannot see condition, renovation, view or finish, and a market with few sales in a year gives a wide range for that year. No indicated, reconciled or opinion-of-value figure is produced.
